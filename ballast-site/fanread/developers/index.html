The outside layer, as data.

A team's systems each know the same fan in part: the venue, the shop, the app. Most of what a fanbase does happens somewhere else, on YouTube, TikTok, Reddit, podcasts and prediction markets. This feed carries that part, per team, every week, in a shape a fan platform can load.

The feed

One JSON file per team, refreshed every week: https://ballast.la/fanread/feed/<team>.json. Example: new-york-knicks.json. Schema: schema.json (v0.1).

teamstringTeam name.
as_ofdateDate of the full read.
summarystringTwo sentences on the fanbase right now.
segments[]objectFan types outside the team's systems: id, name, who, size_signal, where, enjoys, offer, evidence (URLs).
players[]objectPlayers fans follow, ranked: name, wikipedia_views_30d, wikipedia_views_year, url.
formats[]objectWhat fans watch, by share of views, with example URLs.
make_or_reward[]objectFormats a team should make or reward, each with the number behind it.
talk[]objectThreads fans are arguing about, with URLs.
pulseobjectThis week vs last: players rising (views, % change), most-watched videos, top threads, team page views.

Today it's a file per team. It can also arrive as a webhook when something moves, or be written straight into your store, whichever you'd rather.

How it meets a fan

The feed is public, aggregate data about a fanbase, never about a person. It meets a person only inside the fan's own profile, when they link an account (TikTok, YouTube, Reddit) and say yes. Then the platform can place them in an outside segment and count what they do there.
