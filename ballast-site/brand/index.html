Brand system,
derived from the mark

The mark's own numbers were measured off the logo file — the oblique, the ellipse ratio, the outline weight. The rest of the system was built on top of them and is documented here as the site actually runs it, not as it was first imagined.

01

The Mark

Measured, not estimated
PropertyValue
Oblique angle23° from vertical
Lockup ratio2.37 : 1
Ellipse ratio1.46 : 1
Outline weight5.4% of mark height
ConstructionWhite fill, hard outline, radial ground
Minimum width160px digital / 34mm print
Clear spaceOne ellipse height on every side

The 23° oblique is the single most distinctive property. It is steep — roughly twice a normal italic — and it is why the mark reads as motion. Nothing else in the system leans at 23°; if a second element does, the mark stops being the thing that leans.

The lockup is the wordmark and its ellipse together. They are never separated: the ellipse is not a background shape, a pattern element, or a page ground. wordmark.png — the type without the ellipse — exists for cobalt and dark grounds only.

The mark itself carries its own sampled ramp — amber core #F49313, orange at 74% #F76A08, warm rim #F68F0C — which is why the ellipse glows. The UI accents run slightly hotter than the mark; that is deliberate, so type and chips read at small sizes.

The page is not all paper. It runs paper → ink → gradient → cobalt in a deliberate rhythm, so no two heavy sections sit on the same ground twice in a row.

03

The Ground

Blooms, never a bullseye
Right

Four radial blooms at different centres over a diagonal base that sinks into deep water. Off-axis, asymmetric, glowing. This is the hero and sprint ground, verbatim.

Wrong

A concentric radial is a bullseye — a copy of the mark's own ellipse, competing with the thing standing in front of it. Never behind the mark, never as a field.

Orange never appears as a flat fill at panel scale. Both gradient grounds carry a coarse film-grain overlay at low opacity — the thing that stops CSS reading as CSS. That grain and the fixed 5% paper grain are the only textures in the system.

04

Typography

Archivo, one family

Display oblique

Display upright

Section heading

Body copy sits at 400–500 weight, 16px, 1.62 leading. Long enough to say something, short enough that nobody has to work for it. Never justified, never centred below three lines.

Data · labels · codes · IBM Plex Mono

One family does the work. Archivo at 900 italic rhymes with the mark without competing with it; at 800–900 upright it carries headings; at 400–500 it reads as body. Archivo Narrow at 600–700, letterspaced and uppercase, is the fine-print voice. Plex Mono is the only second family, and only for data, codes and section numbers. A light serif was tried and is wrong: it descends from a different century than the mark and makes the logo look like a sticker somebody left on an essay.

05

Geometry

The lozenge and the die-cut
The lozenge — 1.46 : 1

The ellipse from the mark is the only organic shape in the system. It appears at 1.46:1, never circular, never blobbed, always carrying the ramp — and only as part of the lockup or as a deliberate specimen like this one.

The die-cut
Pressed on hover

Everything interactive is cut out the way the wordmark is: 2px ink outline, square corners, hard offset shadow at 4px, and a physical press on hover — the element moves into its own shadow. No soft shadows, no rounded corners, no glows.

07

Rules

Learned the hard way

Do

  • Let the mark be the loudest thing on any surface it appears on.
  • Keep the wordmark and its ellipse together. Always.
  • Build orange grounds from off-centre blooms sinking into deep water.
  • Outline at 2px ink, square corners, offset shadow, press on hover.
  • Set data in Plex Mono, fine print in Archivo Narrow, everything else in Archivo.
  • Let packaging voice label the structure — Warnings, Serving Suggestion, lot codes — while body copy talks like a person.

Don't

  • Strip the ellipse off the mark, or echo it as a background shape.
  • Fill anything panel-sized with flat orange, or any concentric radial.
  • Pair the mark with a light editorial serif.
  • Move anything except the booked light, the button press and the tape.
  • Explain the joke. If a line explains the line above it, cut it.
  • Lean anything else at the mark's 23°.
