How to choose

  1. Discipline fit: AV and trailers, key art, social creative, paid media buying, or digital builds. Most LA entertainment shops don't buy media.
  2. Title track record: recent named titles by format (theatrical, streaming series, games) and by studio.
  3. Paid media capability: whether they plan and buy media themselves or hand creative to a separate media agency.
  4. Audience reach: multicultural and Spanish-language work, international markets and fandoms.
  5. Speed and versioning: localization, cut-down and versioning capacity for launch windows.
  6. Measurement: whether they report on tune-in, subscriptions or ticket sales, or only on views.
  7. Location and access: an LA office near the studios, and time-zone coverage for global releases.

Common questions

What's the difference between a trailer house, a creative agency and a paid media agency?
Trailer and AV houses cut trailers and promos. Creative agencies make key art, social and campaign ideas. Paid media agencies plan and buy the ads that put that work in front of audiences. Big launches often use all three; smaller releases often need one partner that covers media and copy.
Which LA entertainment agencies also plan and buy paid media?
On this list: Ballast, Giant Spoon and PETROL list media planning and buying. Most of the others focus on creative production.
How far before release should paid media start?
Awareness usually builds over the weeks before release, with spend concentrated around the trailer, the release date and opening weekend or premiere week. The right window depends on the title, the budget and the platform.
How do agencies reach multicultural, Spanish-language or international audiences?
Through transcreated creative (not just translation), targeting built for each audience, and partners who know those communities. Ask for named examples.
How is success measured for an entertainment launch?
Views and engagement are easy to report. Stronger measures tie spend to outcomes: ticket sales, tune-in, subscriptions or wishlists for games. Ask which outcome the agency reports against.
Can one agency handle both the creative and the media for a launch?
Some can. If you want one partner, look for an agency that lists both creative and media planning and buying, and ask who actually writes and who actually buys.
