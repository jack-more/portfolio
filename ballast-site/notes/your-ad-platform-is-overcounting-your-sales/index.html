Your ad platformis overcountingyour sales

An ad platform claimed more than twice the sales the store could prove. Here’s where the gap came from, what we run spend on now, and a ten-minute check for your own account.

Jack Morello · Ballast · November 2026 · 7 min read

We were scaling a DTC client with several brands across Meta, TikTok and Google. Spend kept going up and the dashboards kept looking great. The money in the bank added up to a lot less.

Every budget call was being made off those dashboards, so before adding another dollar we audited the whole setup, from the pixel to the invoices.

01The audit

Six places the gap came from

01

The platform credits itself

View-through conversions, a 7-day click window, people who came back days later on a different device, and repeat customers who were going to reorder anyway. The platform counted every one of them as an ad sale.

02

One order, three purchases

The site’s pixel, a server event and an old tag-manager tag nobody remembered adding all fired Purchase for the same order.

03

A tag container that never ran

It loaded from an address that didn’t resolve. The setup everyone thought was live had never fired once.

04

Express checkout skipped the count

Express checkout jumped past the confirmation page, and that page was the only place the purchase event fired. Analytics saw about a third of real orders.

05

Sources got stripped

Orders from Instagram, YouTube and influencer links lost their UTMs at checkout, so every channel but one looked dead.

06

Spend came from the wrong place

The tracking tool reported 23% less spend than the ad accounts actually billed, which made every return look better than it was.

One thing was fine

Click-ID capture sat around 83%, which is healthy once in-app browsers take their cut. It took ten minutes to check and kept us from spending a week on the wrong problem.

02The fix

What we run spend on now

Rule 1The store is the scoreboard

Every order is tied to its campaign, ad set and ad in our own order data. Platform numbers still help the algorithms optimize. We grade the work on the store.

Rule 2Spend comes from invoices

The number we divide by is what the ad accounts actually billed, pulled from the invoices.

Rule 3Reconcile every week

Platform-claimed purchases next to verified new customers, by channel. The ratio between the two is the number that matters.

Rule 4Shorter windows on new campaigns

Switching to a 1-day click window makes reported conversions drop overnight. Sales stay the same; the counting changed. Tell leadership before you flip it.

Rule 5Budget follows real CAC

New customers per week against spend, by ad. Creator pay and channel mix follow the same verified ratio.

03Your account

The ten-minute check

Try this week

  1. Count last week’s store orders you can tie to each platform. Compare with what each platform claims.
  2. Open your tag manager in preview mode on the live site. Make sure the container you think is running actually fires.
  3. Place a test order through express checkout. Check that analytics records it.
  4. Click your own Instagram ad and buy something. Check that the order keeps its source.
  5. Line up last month’s ad invoices against the spend your tracker reports.

If any of those come back wrong, it’s usually a quick fix, and it changes every budget decision after it. This is the setup the client scaled on, to more than $1.2M a month in sales at a peak 6× ROAS.
