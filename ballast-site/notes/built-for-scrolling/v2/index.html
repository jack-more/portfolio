Fandom · Ballast Notes №2

Built for scrolling

The feed can get anyone to watch anything. Getting them to care was never its job. This note is built to be scrolled, so every number shows up as something you can see.

Jack Morello · Ballast · November 2026
Scroll slowly
01 · The hookTikTok knows how long it takes to hook someone. In internal documents from Kentucky’s lawsuit, its own people put the number at 260 videos.1 Watch the phone fill as you scroll.
At the length of a typical clip, that’s about 35 minutes. There’s no public number for how long it takes to care about the person in one.
02 · The feed

Your friends left the feed

Each square is a slice of your time on Instagram. The blue ones are friends. In 2023 it was 11%. By 2025, Meta’s own trial exhibit put it at 7%.3
It works if the goal is time. When researchers moved people off the algorithmic feed, time on the apps dropped. Algorithmic Instagram users spent 107% more time than average; chronological users, 84%.4
03 · The loop

Same five things, on repeat

Across 4.9 million TikTok videos watched by 347 real people, the feed settled on what it knew about each person within about 100 videos.5 Then it kept coming back around.
Trying to steer it barely works. Across 567 million YouTube recommendations, pressing Dislike cut similar ones by 12%. Not interested, 11%.7
04 · Music

The clip eats the fan

Out of every 100 people who found an artist, here’s how many went on to listen to more of their music, depending on where they found them.11
Spotify streams a song picks up per TikTok post at the moment it goes viral. Watch it fall from 2020 to 2025.12 “The itch is for returning to the moment, the snippet.”
05 · Sports

Free agents

How much of their sports viewing is live games. Fans over 60: 60%. Fans 18 to 29: 25%. The rest is clips and highlights.13 38% of Gen Z have no favorite team.14
06 · The split

Two kinds of fan

One in five US music listeners is a superfan.16 Watch them separate from the crowd. They spend $113 a month on live music against $68.15
07 · The fix

Recruit on the feed. Keep them somewhere else.

Every case that held up did the same thing: used the feed to get noticed, then gave people a reason to come somewhere the feed doesn’t control.
The Eras Tour sold 10.17M tickets.20 Weverse fans spend 263 minutes a month in a room the artist owns.22 F1 sold a whole cast and grew US fans 10.5%.25 Feastables made $251M while the videos lost money.29

What you just read

  1. The feed gets your attention fast and almost never your loyalty.
  2. Fandom is splitting: a huge base stuck in the clip, a small core going deep.
  3. Use the feed to recruit. Keep people somewhere you own, and measure who moves.
Try this weekPull your 10 most-viewed posts from the last 90 days. Count the people who did something after watching: signed up, followed you somewhere you own, bought, came back. Divide by views. That’s your real conversion from viewer to fan.
