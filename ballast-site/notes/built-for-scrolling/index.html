Fandom · Ballast Notes №2

Built forscrolling

The feed can get anyone to watch anything. It was never designed to make anyone care. What the research since 2021 says about fandom in the feed era, and how to build fans that last.

Jack Morello · Ballast · November 2026 · 12 min read

TikTok knows how long it takes to hook someone. In internal documents that surfaced in Kentucky’s lawsuit against the company, its own people put the number at 260 videos.1
At the length of a typical clip, state officials worked out, that’s about 35 minutes. Less than one episode of a sitcom.
There’s no public number for how many videos it takes to actually care about the person in one. As far as the public record shows, nobody is measuring that.

01The feed

Your friends left the feed

Ten years ago the feed was the people you chose. Today it’s mostly people you didn’t.

In 2023 Meta told investors that more than a fifth of what people saw on Facebook and Instagram came from accounts they didn’t follow.2 By the time Meta was defending itself in the FTC’s antitrust trial in 2025, its own exhibit showed time spent on friends’ content had fallen to 17% on Facebook and 7% on Instagram.3

“Friends” is narrower than every account you follow, so followed content is somewhat higher. The direction is the point.

It works, if the goal is time. In a three-month experiment run with Meta during the 2020 US election, researchers took about 44,000 Facebook and Instagram users and moved some of them from the algorithmic feed to a plain chronological one. Time on the apps dropped.4

02The loop

Same five things, on repeat

If your feed feels like the same handful of things over and over, you’re reading it right.

Researchers who collected 4.9 million TikTok videos watched by 347 real people found the For You feed settles on what it knows about you within roughly the first 100 videos. After correcting for noise, 30% to 50% of what it serves is aimed straight at interests it already has on file.5 When the Wall Street Journal ran more than 100 bots through TikTok in 2021, one built to linger on sad videos ended up with a feed that was 93% sadness and depression after 224 videos, about 36 minutes in.6

Trying to steer it barely works. Mozilla studied 22,722 people and more than 567 million YouTube recommendations to see how well the buttons work.7

YouTube disputed the method, saying it “doesn’t take into account how our systems actually work.”

The platforms know. In 2023 TikTok added a button to wipe your For You feed for when recommendations “no longer feel like they’re for you.”8 Instagram added its own reset in 2024, and Adam Mosseri warned it was “a big thing to do” and that the app would be less engaging right after.9

One distinction matters. The best research on politics finds YouTube’s recommender doesn’t drag people toward the extremes; if anything it pulls them back toward the middle.10 The loop in entertainment is quieter than radicalization. It finds what works on you and keeps serving it. That’s comfortable, forgettable, and exactly what keeps you scrolling.

03Music

The clip eats the fan

Music is where you can see the cost most clearly.

MIDiA surveyed more than 10,000 people in 2025. Nearly half, 48%, hadn’t streamed a single song they’d heard on social media in the past month. Fewer than a third became fans of the artists.11 Then it looked at what happened after people found an artist.

Out of every 100 people who found an artist… MIDiA Research, 2025

26followed them on TikTok, then listened to more of their music

People aged 16 to 24 drop off at almost every step, and one of their most common reasons for not streaming is that they already hear the song enough on social.

“The itch is for returning to the moment – the snippet – not the song or the artist.”
Tatiana Cirisano, MIDiA Research11

Chartmetric’s data shows the squeeze from the other side. In 2020 it took a song about 340 days to reach 100,000 TikTok posts. By 2025 it took about 48. Over the same stretch, the Spotify streams a song picked up per TikTok post at the moment it went viral fell by almost two thirds.12

04Sports

Free agents

Sports used to be where loyalty got handed down. You watched the whole game because that was the only way to see it.

Fans aged 18 to 29 now spend a quarter of their sports viewing time on live games. Fans over 60 spend 60%. The rest goes to highlights, clips and video games.13

38% of Gen Z say they have no favorite team, against 25% of all US adults. 63% say content from athletes makes them more engaged.14 And young fans are more avid than older ones about the NBA, UFC and WNBA.13 They care plenty. They follow players, formats and moments, and they move when those move.

05The split

Two kinds of fan

Put it together and fandom splits in two: a huge base that meets you in the clip and stays there, and a smaller core that goes all the way in.

Luminate counts about one in five US music listeners as superfans.16 They spend far more than everyone else.15

Gen Z and millennials make up 63% of the most engaged superfans, and US CD sales rose 16% in the first half of 2026.16 About half of Gen Z and millennials tell Deloitte they feel more connected to social creators than to TV and film actors, and their six-month churn on streaming services runs above 50%.17 The loyalty went to people. Subscriptions get dropped casually.

The shared screen is mostly gone too. YouTube is now the biggest single place Americans watch TV, at 13.8% of viewing time, ahead of Netflix at 8%.18 New TV premieres in the first half of 2026 fell to 517, from 930 in the same stretch of 2022.16 The big exception is live sport: Super Bowl LX drew 125.6 million people.19

06The fix

Recruit on the feed. Keep them somewhere else.

The feed is a great place to meet people and a bad place to keep them. Every case that held up in the research did some version of the same thing: it used the feed to get noticed, then gave people a reason to come somewhere the feed doesn’t control.

Move 1Give them an appointment

10.17MEras Tour tickets
$2.08BGross

Fandom forms around things you have to show up for. The Eras Tour sold 10.17 million tickets and grossed $2.08 billion.20 Kai Cenat became the first Twitch streamer past one million active subscribers during his Mafiathon 3 subathon.21 How many stayed after it ended hasn’t been published. Live sport is the last big shared screen for the same reason.

For a brand it can be small: a weekly drop, a monthly live, a show with a schedule people learn.

Move 2Own the room, and show up in it

263Min per user per month, Weverse
1M+Dropout subscribers

HYBE says fans on Weverse, where K-pop artists post and message fans directly, spend about 263 minutes a month there, and that using the artist DM and live features made about 15% of users more active.22 Those are HYBE’s own numbers. Dropout, the comedy streamer, passed one million paying subscribers on its own platform, then added a $129.99-a-year Superfan tier people pay extra for by choice.23

An email list, an app, a Discord, a membership. Somewhere the algorithm doesn’t decide who sees you.

Move 3Build a cast

−40%WNBA playoffs after the Fever exit
+10.5%F1 US fans, 2024

Interest that rests on one star moves when the star does. WNBA playoff viewing fell more than 40% once Caitlin Clark’s Fever were out.24 F1, which sells a whole grid of drivers and rivalries, grew to 52 million US fans in 2024.25 A year after Taylor Swift started showing up at Chiefs games, YouGov counted more than 4 million additional female NFL fans.26

For a brand: more than one face, more than one creator, recurring characters people can pick a favorite from.

Move 4Give fans something to do

$70M→$750MStanley revenue, four years

Peer-reviewed work on influencer-driven loyalty finds engagement is what carries it. The parasocial bond amplifies it, and can flip into a sense of betrayal when the brand breaks the trust.27 Stanley went from about $70 million to more than $750 million in annual revenue in four years by seeding creators and running limited drops people chased.28

Move 5Sell next to the content

$251MFeastables sales, 2024
−$80MMrBeast media, 2024

MrBeast’s media business lost about $80 million in 2024. His chocolate brand, Feastables, made about $251 million in sales and more than $20 million in profit.29 The attention lived on the feed. The money showed up in something fans could buy and hold.

07What we do now

Judge the feed by who moves

  • Every campaign gets a destination off the feed. A list, an app, a community, a live show, a drop.
  • We report on moves. People who took one step deeper (signed up, followed somewhere we own, bought, came back) per 1,000 viewers.
  • We build a cast. Several creators paid on what they sell, so no single face carries the brand.

Try this week

Pull your 10 most-viewed posts from the last 90 days. For each one, count the people who did something after watching: signed up, followed you somewhere you own, bought, or came back to the site. Divide by views.

That’s your real conversion from viewer to fan. It’s rarely a comfortable number.

One honest limit: no public study yet follows people from the clip that found them to whether they’re still fans years later. TikTok doesn’t give researchers that kind of access. Everything above comes from large surveys, platform documents, court records and case studies, and it all points the same way.
